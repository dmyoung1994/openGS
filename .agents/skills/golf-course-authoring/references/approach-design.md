# Green approach and hole continuity

Design the final-shot corridor, green, and recovery ground as one system. A
putting surface must feel generated by the route and landform, not pasted into
an unrelated surface mask.

## Approach brief

Record before shaping:

```yaml
target_topology: connected | island
final_shot_origins: preferred, neutral, conservative
approach_widths_m: each player class and route
entries: preferred, neutral, safe; aerial or running
surface_sequence: landing area through green
tie_ins: landforms continuing across mowing lines
miss_map: short, long, left, right lies and recovery shots
framing: hazards and vegetation with protected sightlines
island_justification: required only for island topology
```

## Connected targets

- Carry at least one maintained-ground route from the main hole into the green
  complex. Use fairway tongues, rough shoulders, collars, short-grass aprons,
  collection areas, bunkers, and broad contour tie-ins deliberately.
- Make position matter. The preferred side of the preceding landing area must
  earn a clearer view, wider effective target, usable ground contour, or easier
  pin angle. A wide fairway with identical approaches from every point is not
  strategic width.
- Preserve a readable entry normally at least 12 m wide. Narrow or close it
  only when the intended shot is explicitly aerial and fair for every target
  player class.
- Continue macro-contours across surface boundaries. Mowing lines may clarify
  lies, but must not substitute for connected ground forms.
- Give at least two distinct recovery families around the green. Do not surround
  every edge with native terrain, identical rough, sand, or uniformly repelling
  slopes.
- Use foliage to frame depth, preferred angle, and backdrop. Keep it out of
  entries, recoveries, hazard sightlines, and the flag backdrop. Vegetation
  cannot repair missing golf ground.

## Island targets

An island is a valid deliberate premise, not a fallback for disconnected
authoring. Declare `target_topology: island` and require:

- a target and hazard edge visible from every final-shot origin;
- fair carries for casual, skilled, and tour tees or layups;
- a coherent water, quarry, canyon, rooftop, or site story;
- an explicit bailout, drop, or replay rule and legible miss outcomes;
- scale sufficient for intended dispersion and wind;
- no fake island made only by an accidental ring of native surface.

## Transferable architectural principles

- **C. B. Macdonald and Seth Raynor:** adapt strategic ideas to the site rather
  than copying shapes. Use generous width to reward the correct position, and
  keep ground approaches available when the concept supports them.
- **Alister MacKenzie:** offer routes for different abilities; use slopes and
  broad tie-ins to connect fairway, approach, green, and context. Preserve
  pleasure, visibility, natural beauty, and optional risk.
- **H. S. Colt and C. H. Alison:** let hollows, ridges, ravines, and hazards
  direct play. Every constructed feature must belong to the landform.
- **George Thomas and A. W. Tillinghast:** hazards and angles should reveal a
  problem, not decorate empty space. Remove features that add cost without a
  golfing decision.
- **Donald Ross:** build varied recovery ground around readable green edges;
  do not reduce crowned greens to isolated domes.

Use principles only. Never trace a named hole, green, bunker plan, or visual
identity.

## Failure patterns

- Green mask surrounded by native terrain with no maintained approach.
- Fairway stopping shortly before green without a strategic forced carry.
- Rough collar of uniform width and outcome around every edge.
- Decorative bunkers or foliage used to conceal an empty transition.
- Preferred and safe routes merging into the same approach problem.
- Island target without declared premise, scaled carry, or penalty recovery.
