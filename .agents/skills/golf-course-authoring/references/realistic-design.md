# Realistic course design

Create an original course that appears buildable, maintainable, and native to its site.

- Start with topography, drainage, wind, soil, vegetation, access, and intended player population.
- Route for varied direction, elevation, par, shot shape, and wind rather than visual symmetry.
- Use width to create angles and choices. Avoid a narrow corridor with hazards symmetrically framing every shot.
- Let maintained turf follow play and drainage. Blend its edges into native landforms.
- Make earthwork broad and continuous. Avoid isolated procedural mounds, noise, impossible retaining slopes, and water without a watershed.
- Scale carries and bailouts across casual, skilled, and tour tees while preserving the hole's central question.
- Vary green contour families, entries, edges, hazards, and recovery palettes across the course.
- Use named courses only for transferable principles. Never trace routing, contours, hazard outlines, or visual identity.

Validate construction logic, surface drainage, visibility, strategic alternatives, physics surfaces, and recovery play.
