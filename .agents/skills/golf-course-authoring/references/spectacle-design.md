# Spectacle course design

Create original, TGL- and video-game-inspired holes with authentic golf physics and a strong visual premise.

## Method

1. Write a one-sentence hook that a player and spectator understand immediately.
2. Preserve one coherent golf decision beneath the spectacle: line, carry, trajectory, spin, timing, or recovery choice.
3. Build a conservative route and a hero route for casual, skilled, and tour tees.
4. Use one primary spectacle system and at most one supporting system. Do not pile unrelated gimmicks together.
5. Reveal the target, hazard movement, landing outcomes, and score volatility clearly from the playing and cinematic cameras.
6. Keep all gameplay motion driven by simulation time and a fixed seed.

## Systems

- **Wind volume:** a visible bounded air current that modifies aerodynamic wind only inside its volume.
- **Timed gate:** a moving physical opening with a readable period and deterministic collision pose.
- **Waterfall:** a water source and moving visual sheet tied to a real water hazard and drainage story.
- **Scenic motion:** non-colliding environmental animation such as rotating machinery, wildlife silhouettes, lava glow, or moving light.

Preserve normal gravity, ball aerodynamics, spin, bounce, roll, and material contact. Do not use portals, boosters, teleportation, invisible modifiers, or frame-dependent obstacles.

## Composition

- Use impossible scale or location—arena, canyon, quarry, volcanic shelf, rooftop, glacier, ancient ruin—while keeping distances and landing areas legible.
- Give the hook strategic consequence. A spectacular object that never affects aim, flight, lie, or camera is scenery, not design.
- Prefer score volatility from a clear risk/reward decision over arbitrary punishment.
- Design for replay: alternate winds, pins, tee angles, and gate timing should change the preferred solution without changing the hole's identity.
- Keep branding, named TGL holes, and proprietary visual identities out of generated work.
